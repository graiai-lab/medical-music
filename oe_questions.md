7 QUESTIONS OPEN

Each item quotes lines from a mnemonic verse about a chemotherapy or
immunotherapy drug. For each, is every clinical claim in the quoted lines
correct as stated? If a number or statement is wrong, give the correct one.

17. Checkpoint inhibitor colitis: "The ipi burns the colon raw, the stool count climbs all day. So send for C. diff, C.M.V., then steroids, don't delay. If three days pass and nothing turns, infliximab is due, or vedolizumab will do, don't let the scope stall you." Is ipilimumab the checkpoint agent most associated with colitis? Are stool testing for Clostridioides difficile and CMV, then corticosteroids without waiting for endoscopy, and infliximab or vedolizumab for steroid-refractory disease after about 3 days, consistent with current guidelines?

18. Irinotecan: "hits the gut in two ways, fast and slow. The early kind is cramps and sweat, so atropine will go. The late kind comes a week or more, with fever and low counts. High-dose loperamide at home, admit when fever mounts." Is early irinotecan diarrhoea a cholinergic syndrome treated with atropine, and late diarrhoea (after 24 hours, typically days to a week or more) managed with high-dose loperamide, with admission when it coincides with fever or neutropenia?

19. Capecitabine and fluoropyrimidines: "strips the gut, the mouth, the hands, the stool. But if it's savage, early, all, then D.P.D.'s the rule. There is an antidote, but fast, four days from the last dose. It's uridine triacetate." Are mucositis, hand-foot syndrome and diarrhoea the common toxicities; is severe early multi-system toxicity the presentation of DPD deficiency; and is uridine triacetate the antidote, to be started within 96 hours of the last fluoropyrimidine dose?

20. Checkpoint inhibitor hepatitis: "The nivo lifts the liver tests, the A.L.T. will climb. So rule out virus, rule out drugs, grade two, it's steroid time. No infliximab when they fail, it hurts the liver more. It's mycophenolate you add, and biopsy for sure." Is the workup exclusion of viral and other drug causes; are corticosteroids indicated from grade 2; is infliximab avoided in immune hepatitis because of hepatotoxicity, with mycophenolate the usual second agent; and is biopsy recommended in steroid-refractory or atypical cases?

21. Sinusoidal obstruction syndrome / VOD: "The busulfan that preps the graft will clog the liver's veins. Inotuzumab does it too, with weight gain, jaundice, pains. It comes within the first three weeks, the liver's big and sore. Defibrotide is what you give." Are busulfan-based conditioning and inotuzumab ozogamicin (and gemtuzumab) recognised causes; is the classic triad weight gain, painful hepatomegaly and hyperbilirubinaemia within about 21 days of transplant; and is defibrotide the specific treatment for severe disease?

22. Neutropenic enterocolitis: "Cytarabine will wreck the wall with neutrophils at none. Right lower pain and fever both, typhlitis has begun. So C.T., no colonoscope, the wall could tear and bleed. Antibiotics, rest the gut, and surgeons if they need." Is cytarabine (with taxanes) a classic precipitant; is the presentation right lower quadrant pain plus fever in a neutropenic patient; is CT the diagnostic test with endoscopy avoided because of perforation risk; and is management broad-spectrum antibiotics, bowel rest, and surgery only for perforation, uncontrolled bleeding or clinical deterioration?

23. Asparaginase: "clots the veins, the pancreas as well. So lipase, sugar, bili, clot, the four that always tell. In A.L.L. the young get this, the belly pain is true. A sinus thrombosis can strike, a headache's imaged too." Are thrombosis (including cerebral venous sinus thrombosis), pancreatitis, hepatotoxicity with steatosis and hyperbilirubinaemia, and hyperglycaemia the principal asparaginase toxicities; is it used mainly in acute lymphoblastic leukaemia in children, adolescents and young adults; and should new headache prompt venous imaging?
